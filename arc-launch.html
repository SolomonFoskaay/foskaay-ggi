Quick answers (read these first)

(1) What is Foskaay GGI?
Foskaay GGI is short for Foskaay Gasless Games Infrastructure. It lets a game open an on-chain session on Arc, run all moves, dice, timers and point credits for free inside that session, and settle once. Players never pay gas and never see a wallet popup. Your game contract stays the source of truth; the rail only provides the room.
(2) Is this on Arc Mainnet?
Yes, and also on testnet. You can build and test on Arc testnet for free, then connect to mainnet when your game is ready. The same contracts and the same fee model on both.
(3) Is Foskaay GGI open source?
Yes. The contracts, relay, SDK and demo are open at github.com/solomonfoskaay/foskaay-ggi.

01 The problem: Arc games cost a fortune per move

Arc is cheap, fast and stablecoin-powered. USDC is the gas token, so costs are predictable in dollars, which is exactly what game deals, day-one economies and micro-charges need. The thing that stopped me from building on it was not the chain itself. It was the rail.

When I tried to put a proper game on Arc, I found there was no gasless rail like MagicBlock ER. On Arc, every action had to be a transaction. A regular 2-player Ludo is roughly 100 moves per player, so 200+ moves plus dice per game, and every one of those was a sponsor transaction that somebody pays for. I priced the obvious path at about 1 to 3 USDC per single game. No game developer can subsidize that per player, per session. DeFi contracts are set once and left alone; games change constantly, new modes, balance tuning, events. Expensive per-action play simply does not work for games.

02 What I built: the missing rail, for everyone

I am a smart contract developer who works in Solidity (EVM) and Rust (SVM, the Solana world), and I have shipped enough real games to know the blocker was never the game logic. It was the cost of the plumbing around it. So instead of waiting for the rail to appear, I built Foskaay GGI on Arc.

  • One session, two Arc transactions. You connect a session (the fee is paid in USDC once) and settle it when play finishes. That is it. Everything between is free for the player and for you.
  • Moves are not transactions. Each move runs through your own pure contract logic via eth_call, is hash-chained and signed with session keys, and commits to the chain only at settle. A 200-move game costs the same as a 5-move game.
  • Players never touch gas. No wallet popup per move, no signatures, no friction. When the session settles, the player's wallet and points simply exist, on-chain.
  • Your contract stays the source of truth. The rail only owns the room. The game keeps its own economy, leaderboards, items and upgrades, behind upgradeable UUPS proxies so games can iterate without stranded accounts.
The Foskaay GGI midchain is not a sidechain and not a separate validator or RPC. It is a signed, off-chain, hash-chained move log that is anchored on Arc twice, at connect and at settle, so anyone can replay a session and verify every move against the on-chain result. Games stay fully on-chain, just unbelievably cheaper to play.

04 Provably fair, fully on-chain

  • Dice. The seed is committed on-chain at connect and revealed at settle. Every roll is derived from it through your own pure rules, and anyone can re-run it in the Explorer.
  • Crown and points. Winners, placements and points are written straight into the state and landed on-chain at settle. No replay, no he-said-she-said.
  • The frontend only displays. Nothing is invented in the browser, nothing trusted from a server database. The chain and the signed, verifiable session are the only truth.

07 Why Arc needs this now

Arc is known as a finance chain, and beneath great games that is exactly the right foundation. USDC gives stable, predictable matching and payout for game deals and in-game economies, the chain is cheap and fast enough for real play, and the network is building fast. The one thing holding game developers back was the cost of running a game action by action. That door is what Foskaay GGI opens. Games on Arc can now be played cheap, be provably fair, and let developers stop burning subsidy on gas.
